## Q1. Tree and plant species on and around the campus

### Takeaway
A 2024 feature on Trường Đại học Nha Trang names the campus planting explicitly: **xà cừ, me tây and bàng along the main campus roads**, with **phượng (red), bằng lăng (purple), liễu, muồng hoàng yến (yellow), hoa giấy (bougainvillea), "tuyết sơn phi hồng" (purple) and hoa sen (lotus)** as the flowering layer. Hundreds of the trees are over fifty years old, which matches the large round canopies on the official campus map. Notably, **phi lao (casuarina) and dừa (coconut) are NOT documented on campus** — phi lao is documented as a *city/beachfront* signature, not a campus tree.

### Cited Findings — CONFIRMED ON CAMPUS
- The campus is on **đồi Lasan (Lasan hill), Vĩnh Thọ ward**, Nha Trang, and **Nha Trang Bay is visible from the grounds**; the article's framing is "ngôi trường vừa có rừng vừa có biển" (the school that has both forest and sea) — [Dân chủ & Pháp luật, 23/02/2024](https://danchuphapluat.vn/choang-ngop-voi-hang-cay-co-thu-co-tuoi-doi-hon-nua-the-ky-tai-ngoi-truong-vua-co-rung-vua-co-bien-5792.html)
- Campus area **over 20 ha, more than half of it green space**; roughly **10 m² of green space per student/lecturer**; about **1,000 trees planted per year** across the main campus and its workshops/stations/farms, with an annual Tết planting tradition — [same source](https://danchuphapluat.vn/choang-ngop-voi-hang-cay-co-thu-co-tuoi-doi-hon-nua-the-ky-tai-ngoi-truong-vua-co-rung-vua-co-bien-5792.html)
- **Hundreds of trees on campus are over half a century old** ("cây cổ thụ có tuổi đời hơn nửa thế kỷ") — [same source](https://danchuphapluat.vn/choang-ngop-voi-hang-cay-co-thu-co-tuoi-doi-hon-nua-the-ky-tai-ngoi-truong-vua-co-rung-vua-co-bien-5792.html)
- Species named **along the main campus roads: "xà cừ", "me tây", "bàng"** — [same source](https://danchuphapluat.vn/choang-ngop-voi-hang-cay-co-thu-co-tuoi-doi-hon-nua-the-ky-tai-ngoi-truong-vua-co-rung-vua-co-bien-5792.html)
- Also named on campus as shade/flowering species: **"phượng"** (red flowers, tied in captions to the lecture halls), **"bằng lăng"**, **"liễu"** (willow), **"muồng"** / caption "**muồng Hoàng yến**" (yellow blooms filling parts of the yard), **"hoa giấy"** (covering the path past the **G7 building** in summer), **"Tuyết sơn phi hồng"** (purple flowers; the article does not say tree or shrub), and **"hoa sen"** (lotus, in a campus pond/yard) — [same source](https://danchuphapluat.vn/choang-ngop-voi-hang-cay-co-thu-co-tuoi-doi-hon-nua-the-ky-tai-ngoi-truong-vua-co-rung-vua-co-bien-5792.html)
- Lecture halls, library and dormitories are described as sitting **among large, shady old trees** — [same source](https://danchuphapluat.vn/choang-ngop-voi-hang-cay-co-thu-co-tuoi-doi-hon-nua-the-ky-tai-ngoi-truong-vua-co-rung-vua-co-bien-5792.html)

### Cited Findings — species dimensions and flowering
- **Me tây / rain tree.** The Vietnamese name "me tây" is ambiguous. In urban-forestry usage it normally means **Albizia saman (Samanea saman)**, the umbrella rain tree: **15–25 m tall, crown diameter about 30 m, wide symmetrical umbrella-shaped crown**, pinnate leaves with 6–16 diamond-shaped leaflets 2–4 cm long, **leaflets fold in rain and in the evening** ("rain tree", "five o'clock tree"), **sheds leaves during dry periods**, flowers **pinkish with white and red stamens** in heads of ~12–25, thousands of heads can cover the whole tree; Vietnamese names given as *còng, muồng tím, cây mưa* — [Wikipedia, Albizia saman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albizia_saman)
  - Conflict to flag: a Vietnamese herbal source files "Me Tây" under **Pithecellobium dulce (mán đỉa)**, 15–18 m (another source 15–25 m), with narrow panicle inflorescences at branch tips — [trungtamthuoc.com, Me Tây](https://trungtamthuoc.com/duoc-lieu/me-tay). For a 3D scene the **Albizia saman** reading is the one that matches "large round-canopy shade tree" on the campus map.
- **Xà cừ (Khaya senegalensis).** Sources disagree on height: "about 20 m and up / 20–50 m" and another "35–40 m"; described as having a **broad, densely leaved crown**, planted specifically for urban green mass; **flowers roughly May–July, fruit ripens August–October** — [trungtamthuoc.com, Cây xà cừ](https://trungtamthuoc.com/duoc-lieu/cay-xa-cu); [mytour.vn, cây xà cừ](https://mytour.vn/vi/blog/bai-viet/tam-quan-trong-cua-cay-xa-cu-va-cach-cham-soc-hieu-qua.html). Practical note: in Vietnamese cities xà cừ reads as a tall, dark-green, dome-to-irregular crown on a thick buttressed trunk, often with whitewashed lower trunk.
- **Phượng vĩ (Delonix regia).** In central Vietnam it **starts flowering around May and peaks late May to end of June**; Huế sources say "tháng 5 hè về" and "hoa phượng vỹ chỉ nở đến khoảng hết tháng 6" — [mia.vn, mùa hoa phượng Huế](https://mia.vn/cam-nang-du-lich/lim-tim-voi-loat-anh-check-in-mua-hoa-phuong-khi-di-du-lich-hue-2737). Yellow-flowered *phượng vàng* also occurs in the south-central region (Quy Nhơn, Lâm Đồng) — [VnExpress, phượng vàng](https://vnexpress.net/phuong-vang-ruc-ro-o-lam-dong-4566546.html)
- **Bằng lăng (Lagerstroemia speciosa).** Flowers in summer; Hà Nội sources put the purple bloom in **May** — [Tuổi Trẻ, mùa hoa bằng lăng](https://tuoitre.vn/mua-hoa-bang-lang.html). No central-Vietnam-specific month found.

### Cited Findings — TYPICAL OF NHA TRANG (city, not campus)
- **Phi lao (casuarina)** is treated as *the* signature Nha Trang street/beach tree, and the city's tree company deliberately shapes each one into a sculpted shade canopy ("mỗi cây phi lao hiện diện ở Nha Trang đều là một công trình nghệ thuật mà các công nhân viên Công ty cây xanh đã dày công kiến tạo") — [Tạp chí Công Thương, "Nhớ Nha Trang"](https://tapchicongthuong.vn/tag-lop-xe-11312.htm)
- **Bàng** is described in the same literary register with a "bao la thoáng đạt" (broad, open, airy) canopy, contrasted with other coastal trees — [Báo Mới, "Mùa bàng nõn"](https://baomoi.com/mua-bang-non-c38162561.epi)
- **Nho biển (Coccoloba uvifera, sea grape)** is planted as a Nha Trang coastal hedge/row species — [Báo Mới, "Hàng nho biển"](https://baomoi.com/hang-nho-bien-c35024870.epi)
- City greenery is thin and under pressure: **park greenery only 0.56 m²/person in 2018**, officials saying the city effectively has no real park left and the Trần Phú coastal strip is being squeezed by construction — [Nhân Dân, "Giữ không gian cho TP biển Nha Trang"](https://nhandan.vn/giu-khong-gian-cho-tp-bien-nha-trang-post611955.html). This makes the NTU campus a genuine green island against a largely hard-surfaced city.
- **Typhoon No. 12 (Damrey, 2017) destroyed much of Nha Trang's street tree cover**, uprooting old trees on Yersin and Hai Bà Trưng; the report claims near-total felling on many streets ("đa số các tuyến đường trong thành phố 100% cây xanh đều bị đổ ngã") — [Sài Gòn Giải Phóng](https://www.sggp.org.vn/bao-so-12-pha-tan-mang-xanh-nha-trang-480350.html). Treat the "100%" as rhetorical.
- A 2012 city decision (**Quyết định 5231/QĐ-UBND**) approved a project to adjust street and park tree species in Nha Trang and to preserve heritage old trees tied to the city's history — [Nhân Dân](https://nhandan.vn/giu-khong-gian-cho-tp-bien-nha-trang-post611955.html)

### Inferences
- The campus's "many large round-canopy shade trees" on the official map are most plausibly **me tây (Albizia saman)** — the only named species with a 30 m umbrella crown — with **xà cừ** supplying the taller, darker, more irregular domes and **bàng** the layered, horizontally-tiered pagoda crowns. A foliage kit of three canopy archetypes (wide flat umbrella / tall dark dome / horizontal tiers) plus one palm covers most of the scene.
- **Bàng (Terminalia catappa)** is the one species that gives seasonal leaf colour in this climate: it reddens and drops in the dry months and flushes bright yellow-green ("bàng nõn") on refoliation, which is a cheap, highly legible seasonal cue. The "mùa bàng nõn" article title confirms the phenomenon is locally recognised, though it is a literary piece, not a phenological record.
- **Me tây leaflets folding at dusk and in rain** is a free, authentic animation beat for the dusk and rainy-season presets.
- **Phi lao and dừa should sit on the shoreline/beachfront band of the scene, not inside the campus**, since the documentation puts casuarina on the city's coastal strip and never names either on campus.

### Gaps
- No ntu.edu.vn page or official campus tree inventory was located; the single rich campus source is one 2024 news feature. No per-species counts, no individual tree heights or ages on campus.
- "Tuyết sơn phi hồng" is not a standard botanical Vietnamese name and could not be resolved to a species. It is probably an ornamental (possibly a *Tibouchina*/*Melastoma*-type purple shrub), but this is unverified — do not model it as a named species.
- No month given for bằng lăng or me tây flowering in Khánh Hòa specifically.
- Whether **dừa (coconut)** grows on the NTU campus: not documented either way.

## Q2. Nha Trang climate and light at 12.25° N

### Takeaway
A tropical savanna climate with an unusually long **dry season ~January–August** and a short, sharp **wet season ~September–December**; ~2,539 sunshine hours/year, peaking March–May. Day length barely moves (11h17m to 12h43m), but the **noon sun passes through the zenith twice a year (~23 April and ~19–20 August)**, so midday shadows collapse to almost nothing in April and August and only reach ~54° elevation at the December solstice.

### Cited Findings — climate
- **Tropical savanna climate; long dry season about January to August, shorter wet season September to December** — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang_(city))
- **Annual rainfall ~1,300 mm in the body text, 1,399 mm in the climate table**; **October ~312 mm and November ~382 mm are the wettest**; **February the driest at ~16 mm** — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang_(city))
- **Mean daily highs ~27 °C (January) to ~33 °C (June); mean daily lows ~21 °C (January) to ~26 °C** in the warm months; records ~14.6 °C to ~39.5 °C — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang_(city))
- **Humidity ~79% annual mean; ~2,539 sunshine hours/year, most in March–May** — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang_(city))
- Conflicting sunshine figure: the **Khánh Hòa provincial tourism department gives ~2,380 annual sunshine hours** — [Khánh Hòa DCST, climate overview](https://dulich.khanhhoa.gov.vn/en/introduce/overview/climate.html)
- Conflicting rainfall figures exist across travel-climate aggregators (e.g. 288 mm October / 281 mm November / 16 mm February in one dataset; 330 mm November in another; 2,153 mm annual over ~222 rain days in a third; and a weather2travel June page citing 242 mm against 54 mm in its own main table) — [weather2travel, Nha Trang climate](https://www.weather2travel.com/climate-guides/vietnam/nha-trang.php); [weathermonth.com, Nha Trang](https://weathermonth.com/en/best-time-to-visit/nha-trang). **These aggregators disagree badly; prefer the Wikipedia/NCHMF-derived table and treat aggregator monthly numbers as unreliable.**
- **Typhoons** can bring extremely heavy rain in the wet season, but the bay's **ring of mountains on three sides plus one large and four smaller offshore islands shelters the city from the strongest winds** — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang_(city))
- Cyclone season for Khánh Hòa province waters: a peer-reviewed study of the Trường Sa region (part of Khánh Hòa) found two seasons, with the **main season September to the following January** at roughly **four times** the frequency of the secondary (March-onward) season, coinciding with the strong **Northeast monsoon** — [Vietnam Journal of Marine Science and Technology](https://vjs.ac.vn/index.php/jmst/article/view/14586)
- Provincial tourism guidance labels **September–December "storm season in Central Vietnam — exercise extreme caution"** — [Khánh Hòa tourism portal](https://dulichso.khanhhoa.gov.vn/en/article/when-is-the-best-time-to-visit-khanh-hoa-a98)
- **Typhoon Damrey made landfall in Khánh Hòa in the early hours of 4 November 2017**, described as the strongest typhoon to hit southern Vietnam in 16 years — [PropertyCasualty360 / AIR Worldwide](https://www.propertycasualty360.com/2017/11/07/typhoon-damrey-rocks-vietnam-with-historic-force-air-worldwide-finds/)

### Cited Findings — sun geometry (computed, not sourced)
Computed from standard solar-declination geometry for **lat 12.2529° N, lon 109.1908° E** (Cooper declination approximation, geometric sunrise/sunset, no refraction or elevation correction; expect ±a few minutes on day length and ±0.5° on elevation):

| 21 Feb | 11h40m | 66.5° |
| 21 Mar | 11h59m | 77.3° |
| 21 Apr | 12h20m | 89.3° |
| 21 May | 12h37m | 82.1° |
| 21 Jun | 12h43m | 78.8° |
| 21 Jul | 12h37m | 81.8° |
| 21 Aug | 12h21m | 89.5° |

| 21 Oct | 11h39m | 66.0° |
| 21 Nov | 11h23m | 57.3° |

- **Zenith passages (sun exactly overhead at noon): ~23 April and ~19–20 August.**
- Day-length range across the year is only **1h26m** — seasonal light change here is about *cloud and haze*, not day length.
- Nha Trang sits ~**0.6° east of the ICT (UTC+7, 105° E) meridian centre**, so local solar noon is roughly **12:43 clock time** before the equation of time (±~15 min across the year). Sunrise clusters around 05:30–06:30 and sunset around 17:15–18:20 all year.

### Inferences — four lighting presets
- **Dawn (~05:45–06:30).** Sun rises almost due east, i.e. **out of the sea and over Hòn Tre** — the single most "Nha Trang" lighting moment available. Low sun, long shadows westward up the hill, warm key with strong haze/aerial perspective over water, calm sea (land breeze), mist in the valleys. Short twilight: at 12° N civil twilight is ~22 minutes, so dawn is fast — keep the preset's transition tight if time-of-day is animated.
- **Day (11:00–14:00).** In the dry season this is a near-vertical, very high-intensity key (77–89° elevation Mar–Sep): shadows are small, dark, directly under canopies; concrete and asphalt blow out; foliage looks bleached on top and deeply shadowed inside. This is the harshest and most authentic default. Add strong bounce from pale concrete and sand.
- **Dusk (~17:15–18:20).** Sun sets behind the inland mountains to the west, so the hill loses direct sun earlier than the water; the bay keeps a bright, cool-warm sky reflection while the campus falls into skylight-only ambient. Sea breeze is strongest in the afternoon, so animate foliage harder here.
- **Night.** 12° N tropical night: warm low-CRI sodium/LED campus path lighting, a bright city glow to the south-southwest (city centre and Trần Phú strip), **fishing-boat lights scattered on the bay**, and a near-vertical star field with no circumpolar rotation cue.

### Gaps
- No month-by-month **cloud cover percentage** obtained — weatherspark (the best source for this, plus exact day-length/solar-elevation charts) returned HTTP 503. The report writer should retry `weatherspark.com/y/113262/Average-Weather-in-Nha-Trang-Vietnam-Year-Round` for cloud-cover %, wind direction by month and sunrise/sunset tables.
- No monthly sunshine-hours breakdown from the Vietnamese NCHMF was found; the two annual totals (2,539 vs 2,380) conflict and are from different stations/periods (Cam Ranh airport is ~30 km south).
- No per-year count of typhoon landfalls specifically in Khánh Hòa.

## Q3. Sea, coast, and what is on the horizon from this hillside

### Takeaway
The campus sits on Lasan hill directly above **Hòn Chồng** — the two are the same headland, with Hòn Chồng and Hòn Vợ at the foot of the hill's eastern slope. From a campus facing the bay you would see, left-to-right roughly north→south: the **Cô Tiên ridgeline**, the granite boulder clusters of Hòn Chồng/Hòn Vợ below, the open sweep of **Nha Trang Bay** with **Hòn Tre** dominating the southeast horizon, and the city and Trần Phú strip to the south.

### Cited Findings
- **Hòn Chồng and Hòn Vợ lie at the foot of đồi Lasan**, with Hòn Vợ at the eastern foot of the hill; the site is ~3 km northeast of the city centre, and the Lasan hill road is lined with breezy sea-view cafés — [mia.vn, Hòn Chồng](https://mia.vn/cam-nang-du-lich/hon-chong-nha-trang-hon-dao-tho-mong-lau-doi-cua-thanh-pho-bien-xinh-dep-621)
- Hòn Chồng is **granite boulders in two clusters** (Hòn Chồng nearer shore, smaller Hòn Vợ facing east to open ocean), stacked in irregular layers with a paved path through them; from higher up there are **panoramic views over Nha Trang Bay with Hòn Tre Island offshore and the Cô Tiên mountain ridgeline to the north**; the shoreline here is **rough and rocky, not a swimming beach** — the sand beach is just north of the promontory; **Nha Trang's astronomical observatory sits on the hill above** — [localvietnam.com, Hon Chong](https://localvietnam.com/uncategorized/hon-chong/)
- Hòn Chồng is cited as one of the best vantage points for a panorama of the whole seaside city — [mytour.vn](https://mytour.vn/vi/blog/du-lich/top-10-diem-den-ly-tuong-tai-phuong-bac-nha-trang-khanh-hoa-mytour.html)
- **Nha Trang Bay is enclosed by mountains on three sides**, with one large island (Hòn Tre) and four smaller ones offshore; the **Cái River** mouth carries the **Po Nagar (Tháp Bà) towers at its mouth** — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang_(city))
- Nha Trang's coordinates: **12°14′28″N 109°11′28″E** — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang)
- Administrative caveat for any on-screen labels: **Nha Trang ceased to exist as a municipal city on 1 July 2025**; since 16 June 2025 "Nha Trang" is one of 65 new wards/communes of Khánh Hòa province, and Vĩnh Thọ now falls under a reorganised "Bắc Nha Trang" ward — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang_(city)); [Wikipedia, Nha Trang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang)
- Nha Trang's coastline includes a seasonal **green moss bed ("bãi rêu tóc xanh")** exposed on the rocks along Trần Phú — a vivid green film on dark rock at low tide — [Tuổi Trẻ](https://tuoitre.vn/bai-reu-toc-xanh-tren-con-duong-dep-nhat-nha-trang-20190221152922874.htm)

### Inferences — horizon composition from the campus
- **Definitely on the horizon / in view:** Hòn Tre (large, close, SE — it should read as a substantial dark-blue mountainous mass, not a distant smudge), the Cô Tiên ridge (N/NW), Hòn Chồng's granite boulders immediately below and east, the open bay and the city arc to the south.
- **Probably visible but depends on exact vantage and intervening buildings:** the Cái river mouth and the Xóm Bóng fishing-boat anchorage, and the Po Nagar towers on Cù Lao hill — these lie **west/southwest and slightly inland** of Lasan hill, so they would be side-on or behind, not on the sea horizon. The Trần Phú bridge is further south at the river mouth, likely a mid-distance element rather than a skyline one.
- **Should be read as mid-distance set dressing on the water, not horizon:** wooden fishing fleets and the round basket boats *thúng chai*. These are a real and strongly characteristic Nha Trang element, but I found no source in this research documenting their hull colours. Do not state the blue-hull/red-and-yellow-trim scheme as sourced.
- The rocky, boulder-strewn shoreline right under the campus — not sand — is the correct ground treatment for the seaward edge of the scene.

### Gaps
- **No source found describing the colour of Nha Trang bay water or the beach sand** in words or measurements. The "turquoise water, white sand" description is widely assumed but was not confirmed by any source read here.
- No source confirming visibility of Po Nagar / Trần Phú bridge / Cái river mouth *from the NTU campus specifically*. This needs a photo-based check (campus photo galleries, or a line-of-sight check on a terrain map) before being asserted.
- No source on *thúng chai* or fishing-boat livery colours.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- The **eight-month dry season (Jan–Aug) with February at ~16 mm rain** necessarily means unirrigated grass goes straw/ochre for a large part of the year, while **Sep–Dec (~300–380 mm/month)** brings saturated green. A scene with two grass variants keyed to dry/wet season is defensible on climate grounds alone, even without a photographic source.
- Granite bedrock at Hòn Chồng (sourced) implies a granitic/sandy pale soil on the hill rather than the deep red basalt laterite of the Central Highlands. **Do not default to the strong red-earth palette** associated with inland Vietnam — this is uncertain, but the granite finding argues against it.

### Gaps
- Soil colour on Lasan hill: unknown. Needs a geological map of Khánh Hòa or on-site photos.
- Concrete/asphalt tones, campus path materials, and dry-vs-wet grass colour: no sources found.

## Q7. What reads as "Nha Trang" rather than Hanoi or Saigon

### Takeaway
Three sourced things separate it: the **enclosed mountain-ringed bay with Hòn Tre filling the horizon**, the **granite boulder shoreline at the foot of the campus hill**, and the **sculpted phi lao along the seafront** — plus a climate signature (eight-month dry season, near-vertical sun, short violent Sep–Dec wet season) that no northern Vietnamese city has.

### Cited Findings
- **Mountains on three sides, one big island plus four smaller ones** enclosing the bay — a basin-and-island skyline unique to Nha Trang among Vietnam's big coastal cities — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang_(city))
- **Climate contrast:** Nha Trang is tropical savanna with a **long Jan–Aug dry season and a short Sep–Dec wet season** — the inverse phasing of Hanoi's May–Sep summer monsoon and distinct from Saigon's May–Nov rainy season — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang_(city))
- **Phi lao as the deliberately shaped, locally prized signature street tree** — not a Hanoi or Saigon street tree — [Tạp chí Công Thương](https://tapchicongthuong.vn/tag-lop-xe-11312.htm)
- **Nho biển (sea grape)** rows as a coastal planting — another species absent from inland cities — [Báo Mới](https://baomoi.com/hang-nho-bien-c35024870.epi)
- **Granite boulder headlands** (Hòn Chồng/Hòn Vợ) stacked in irregular layers at the shoreline — [localvietnam.com](https://localvietnam.com/uncategorized/hon-chong/)
- **Cham architecture at the river mouth** (Po Nagar/Tháp Bà at the Cái river mouth) — a south-central, not northern or southern, cultural marker — [Wikipedia, Nha Trang (city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nha_Trang_(city))
- **Exposed green moss beds on the shore rocks** — a specifically local seasonal sight on Trần Phú — [Tuổi Trẻ](https://tuoitre.vn/bai-reu-toc-xanh-tren-con-duong-dep-nhat-nha-trang-20190221152922874.htm)
- Campus-specific identity: the **"vừa có rừng vừa có biển"** combination — a dense fifty-year canopy on a hill with the bay visible through it — is how the campus is described in its own press — [Dân chủ & Pháp luật](https://danchuphapluat.vn/choang-ngop-voi-hang-cay-co-thu-co-tuoi-doi-hon-nua-the-ky-tai-ngoi-truong-vua-co-rung-vua-co-bien-5792.html)

### Inferences
- The single strongest at-a-glance cue available to the scene is **framing the bay and Hòn Tre through the gap between two 50-year-old umbrella canopies**, looking down a slope. That combination — mature inland-looking shade canopy plus an enclosed island bay below — does not exist in Hanoi (no sea) or Saigon (no sea, no hills).
- What to **avoid** if the goal is "reads as Nha Trang": flat deltaic ground, a Hanoi-style tube-house street wall, or an open unbounded ocean horizon. The horizon must be closed by island and ridge.
